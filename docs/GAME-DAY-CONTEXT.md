# Signal Window: simpler language and a familiar setting

The teacher found the abstract explanation and demonstration-session story too difficult to follow. The student-facing mission now uses class Game Day throughout: $18 for snacks plus $6 for a prize each round, within a $90 budget. The time model uses 50 minutes, 4 minutes per round, and at least 14 minutes for cleanup. These are separate money and time limits students can picture.

Teaching now uses short sentences: “An inequality shows a limit” and “At most $90 means $90 or less.” The first chapter teaches the signs, connects 18 and 6x to the story, and asks how many whole rounds fit. It does not lead with real numbers, domains or formal solution-set terminology. Later chapters use the same plain language for graphing, negative division and checking several rules. The six-step example uses school carnival entry and ride tickets; the second example keeps four steps with less support.

All chapter questions, both mastery forms, explanations, writing checklists, model responses and the teacher answer key now match the setting. The archive clips remain story rewards. Graphs still include fractional and decimal values where appropriate, and actual game counts use whole numbers. Dividing by a negative still requires reversing the sign.

The numbers and mathematical answers are unchanged: budget x ≤ 12; time x ≤ 9; strict alternate time x < 9; combined whole rounds 6, 7, 8, 9. Six rounds cost $54 and leave 26 cleanup minutes; nine cost $72 and leave 14 minutes. The same question IDs/revision preserve existing saved progress. Previously written responses are not overwritten; students may revise old-context explanations.

Checks cover the short teaching copy, absence of obsolete student-facing simulation terminology, money/time calculations, answer-key validation and the full student flow. The preview is updated and the change is staged for teacher review, not published.
